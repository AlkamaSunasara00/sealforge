## 1. UX & DESIGN STRATEGY: HOW TO PRESENT THE PRODUCTS

### The Dilemma: "Show Only Name & Image vs. Full Details?"
> **Recommendation:** **Use Progressive Disclosure (Scannable Product Cards with Essential Technical Badges + Expandable Full Engineering Details).**

Here is why:
1. **Why "Name & Image Only" fails for Industrial B2B:**
   - Plant engineers, maintenance managers, and procurement heads **never buy rotating equipment on appearance alone**. They require shaft size, pressure ratings, temperature limits, and material compatibility to know if a product fits their machine.
   - **SEO Impact:** Pages with only names and images have virtually zero text. Search engines will not rank the page for high-value technical searches like *"balanced mechanical seal 25 bar"*, *"polymer lined pump PVDF"*, or *"water-lubricated cutlass bearing brass shell"*.
2. **Why a "Wall of Unformatted Raw Text" fails:**
   - Forcing users to scroll through hundreds of rows of raw tables on mobile creates cognitive overload and high bounce rates.
3. **The Perfect Solution (Progressive Disclosure):**
   - **Top Level (The Card):** Clean image render, Product Series / Name, Primary Application badge, and 4 Key Operational Metrics (Shaft Size, Max Pressure, Max Temp, Max Speed/Flow).
   - **Interactive Layer ("View Technical Specifications" button or toggle):** Expands to reveal the full material matrix, engineering limits, design standards (ISO 5199, ANSI B73.1, EN 12756), and interchangeability codes.
   - **Direct Conversion:** A *"Request Quote / Datasheet"* button on every product that pre-fills the enquiry form with that product's code.

#### Category Overview:
SealForge Cutlass Bearings are water-lubricated sleeve bearings designed to provide dependable, low-friction support for rotating shafts in marine propulsion and industrial pump equipment. Resilient fluted rubber linings utilize the pumped water for self-lubrication, cooling, and debris flushing.

---

#### Shell Types & Mechanical Configurations:
1. **Cylindrical Sleeve Shell:**
   - Conventional cylindrical design pressed directly into bearing housings or stern tubes.
   - Designed for cylindrical housings across standard and custom dimensional lengths.
2. **Flanged Shell:**
   - Integrated end flange provides positive axial location and prevents bearing walk-out under heavy thrust.
   - Custom flange hole patterns, bolt circles, and flange thicknesses available to customer drawings.

#### Shell Material Selection:
- **Metallic Shells:** Provide structural rigidity, press-fit retention, and dimensional stability:
  - Naval Brass
  - Marine Bronze / Gunmetal
  - Stainless Steel (304 / 316) & Mild Steel
  - High alloys available on request.
- **Non-Metallic Shells:** For weight reduction, zero galvanic corrosion, and electrical isolation:
  - Phenolic Resin Composites
  - Engineered Reinforced Thermoset Plastics
  - Fiberglass-Reinforced Laminates.

#### Internal Rubber Lining Profiles & Hydrodynamic Actions:
| Lining Profile | Geometry Description | Key Hydrodynamic Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluted Profile** | Straight longitudinal grooves along full bearing length | Maximum water flow, rapid heat dissipation, and continuous flushing of sand, grit, and abrasive silt particles. |
| **Spiral Profile** | Helically arranged water grooves through bearing bore | Controlled water movement and positive lubrication path along high-speed shafts. |
| **Circular Profile** | Continuous smooth circular internal geometry | Uniform, unbroken shaft support for low-speed or specialized hydraulic duties. |
| **Polygon Profile** | Multi-flat polygonal internal land geometry | Application-specific load distribution and pressure wedge development for high radial loads. |

- **Custom Engineered-to-Order:** Non-standard wall thicknesses, custom lengths, split-shell configurations, and special clearances manufactured to client drawings.

### SECTION 5: APPLICATION-BASED MATERIAL SELECTION GUIDE

*Derived directly from Section 2 of `catalogue design.pdf`.*

#### 1. Primary Sealing Face Material Guide
| Face Material | Description & Grade | Typical Advantages | Recommended Applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbon Graphite (Resin Impregnated)** | Synthetic graphite impregnated with synthetic resin | Self-lubricating, low friction, excellent running properties | Clean water, mineral oils, fuels, general industrial liquids |
| **Carbon Graphite (Antimony Impregnated)** | Synthetic graphite with molten antimony metal | Improved mechanical strength, thermal conductivity, and wear resistance | Higher duty water/oil service, hot hydrocarbons, higher pressures |
| **Alumina Ceramic (99.5% Al2O3)** | High-purity sintered aluminium oxide | Extremely hard, cost-effective, chemical resistant | Clean water, domestic pumps, mild process duties |
| **Silicon Carbide (SiC)** | Sintered / Reaction-bonded silicon carbide | Extreme hardness, universal chemical resistance, handles abrasives | Abrasive slurries, chemical process, wastewater, high temperatures |
| **Tungsten Carbide (TC)** | Sintered carbide with nickel/cobalt binder | Exceptional toughness and impact resistance; resists thermal shock | Viscous slurries, mining, sewage, severe mechanical stress |
| **Filled PTFE** | Chemically modified PTFE with glass/carbon filler | Broadest chemical inertness, low friction | Highly aggressive chemical acids where ceramics/metals are attacked |

#### 2. Secondary Elastomer Compatibility Guide
- **NBR (Nitrile):** `-30 °C to +120 °C` — Water, mineral oils, fuels, general industrial fluids. (Avoid: high temp, ozone, ketones).
- **EPDM:** `-40 °C to +150 °C` — Hot water, steam, aqueous solutions, dilute acids/alkalis. (Do NOT use with petroleum oils/hydrocarbons).
- **FKM (Viton):** `-20 °C to +200 °C` — Mineral oils, synthetic fuels, hydrocarbons, acids.
- **PTFE:** `-40 °C to +200 °C` — Universal chemical resistance for aggressive acid duties.
- **FFKM (Perfluoroelastomer):** `-15 °C to +300 °C` — Severe chemical exposure and extreme operating temperatures.

#### 3. Core Selection Rules:
1. Always select sealing faces as a **compatible pair** (e.g., Carbon vs. SiC for clean duties; SiC vs. SiC for abrasive slurry).
2. Never select secondary seals from temperature alone — verify fluid concentration and chemical compatibility together.
3. Reference standards: Manufactured in alignment with **EN 12756**, **ISO 5199**, and **ANSI B73.1**.
